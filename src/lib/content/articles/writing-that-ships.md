The best marketing writing in construction technology has a tell: it sounds like
it came from someone who has stood in the mud.

You can't fake it. The jargon is easy to borrow, but the specifics are not — the
name of the form the crew hates, the exact moment a schedule slips, the reason a
superintendent ignores your dashboard. Specifics are earned.

## Three habits

1. **Name the real problem.** Not "inefficiency." The thing that made someone
   drive back to the yard at 4pm.
2. **Show the number.** A claim without a figure is a vibe. A figure without a
   source is a guess. Give both.
3. **Cut the throat-clearing.** The first paragraph is almost always warm-up.
   Delete it and start at the second.

> Good writing respects the reader's time. Great writing respects the reader's
> intelligence.

That's the whole game. Write like the person on the other end has a job to do —
because they do.
